# Humanizer

Use this whenever you write or rewrite text the user will send, post, submit or say out loud: school essays and reports, emails, social media captions, blog posts, speeches, applications, messages. The goal is text a reader would never suspect came from a chatbot.

The pattern list comes from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide (WikiProject AI Cleanup). Readers and teachers use the same tells, so avoiding them matters more than any single clever sentence.

## How to work

1. Work out who the reader is and match the register. School and work writing stays formal and keeps any structure the task requires. Personal writing stays plain and natural.
2. Write the draft in plain words. Say the specific thing. If a sentence could fit any topic, cut it or replace it with a concrete fact, number or example.
3. Run the checklist below over the draft and fix every hit.
4. Read it once for rhythm. Mix short and long sentences. Contractions are fine outside formal work.
5. Hand it over with one short question about tone or details. Don't add a summary of what you changed unless asked.

## Checklist: patterns to remove

### Content
- Puffing up importance: tying small details to big themes, "plays a vital/pivotal/crucial role", "a key moment", "stands as a symbol of", "leaves a lasting legacy".
- Piling up claims of fame or coverage instead of saying what sources actually said.
- Trailing "-ing" analysis tacked onto sentences: "..., highlighting its importance", "..., underscoring the need for", "valuable insights".
- Promotional or travel-brochure tone: boasts, nestled, renowned, showcasing, vibrant, rich heritage, breathtaking.
- Vague authorities: "experts argue", "studies show", "many believe", "several sources". Name the source or drop the claim.
- The formulaic ending: "Despite its success, X faces several challenges... Looking ahead, the future is bright."

### Language
- AI vocabulary: delve, tapestry, testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, intricate, landscape (figurative), realm, foster, garner, navigate (figurative), multifaceted, seamless, robust, elevate, embark, journey (figurative), "Additionally" at the start of sentences.
- Dodging "is" and "has": write "is" instead of "serves as" or "stands as", and "has" instead of "boasts" or "features".
- Negative parallelisms: "not only X but also Y", "it's not just X, it's Y", "not X, but Y".
- Rule of three: lists of three adjectives or three short phrases added to sound complete. Use two, or four, or one good one.
- Synonym cycling: calling the same thing by a new name each sentence. Repeat the plain name.

### Style and formatting
- Title Case Headings. Use sentence case.
- Bold sprinkled over key terms.
- Lists where each item starts with a bold label and a colon, when a paragraph would read better.
- Emoji in formal text or before headings and bullets.
- Em dashes used for drama. Prefer commas, full stops, or brackets. One em dash in a piece is fine; several is a tell.
- Small tables that could be a sentence.
- Curly quotes when the destination uses straight ones.
- Email-style subject lines pasted above messages that aren't emails.

### Chatbot leftovers
- "Certainly!", "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like...", "Feel free to..." inside the text itself.
- Knowledge-cutoff talk: "as of my last update", "based on available information".
- Placeholders left in: [Name], [Date], XX.
- Markdown symbols (##, **) in text going somewhere that won't render them, like an email, WhatsApp or a Word doc.
- Made-up or unchecked citations. Every reference must be real and support the claim.

## What good looks like

Before: "Nestled in the heart of Dubai, our studio serves as a vibrant hub of creativity, showcasing innovative, high-quality, and affordable designs, highlighting the transformative power of 3D printing."

After: "We're a small 3D printing shop in Dubai. We print custom parts, gifts and prototypes, and most orders ship within a week."

The second version is shorter, specific and sounds like a person talking.

## Things to keep in mind

- Don't swap one tell for a fancier synonym. Rewrite the sentence.
- Don't strip structure that a school or work task requires. Formal is fine; robotic isn't.
- Keep technical facts accurate while simplifying the wording.
- When given text to humanize, keep the writer's meaning and their own phrases where they already sound natural.
